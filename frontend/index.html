The problem

Pastors have no fast way to check AI.

Churches are starting to use AI for devotionals, sermon outlines, small-group guides and replies to the people they serve. General-purpose AI drifts from a church's doctrine, misquotes or invents verses, and answers hurting people with confident advice.

So pastors either avoid AI altogether, or publish it unreviewed.

  • “It quoted a verse that does not exist.”
  • “It sounds right, but it is not what we teach.”
  • “It told a grieving member to just pray more.”
  • “I do not have time to read everything it writes.”

How it works

From statement of faith to a verdict in four steps.

Your church sets the standard once. After that, anyone can submit content and a pastor only sees what needs a pastor.

01

Set the standard

Upload your statement of faith. Plumbline drafts a short list of commitments, each with the line it came from. A pastor edits and approves them.

02

Submit content

Paste or upload AI-written text, or let another church tool send it through the API.

03

Every claim is checked

The text is split into claims. Each verse is looked up in the Bible text and each claim is judged against your commitments.

04

A pastor decides

You get one verdict with the evidence behind each flag. Doubtful items wait in a review queue for a person.

What it checks

Three checks on every claim.

The verdict engine approves only when all three come back clean.

Scripture check

Every reference is looked up in a public-domain Bible text. Quotes are marked exact, faithful paraphrase, misquote, or reference not found. Invented books are caught too.

Doctrine judge

Each claim is weighed against your own approved commitments and your pastors' past rulings: aligned, conflicts, silent, or contested. It never decides which theology is right.

Risk checks

Crisis language, abuse disclosures, medical or legal advice, replacing professional help, personal information, and content that claims a pastor wrote it.

The verdict

One of three outcomes. Doubt never approves.

If your statement of faith is silent on a claim, or the model is unsure, or the model is down, the item goes to a person. Never to publication.

Approved

Safe to use

Every claim aligns with your commitments and every verse checks out.

Needs pastor review

A human look

A conflict, a silent area, a bad verse, or low confidence. You see the evidence and a suggested fix.

Blocked

Route to a pastor

Crisis or abuse language. Plumbline never writes a reply to a person in crisis. It stops and escalates.

The church's own standard

The same sentence, two churches, two verdicts.

Plumbline is not a generic safety filter. Your statement of faith is the rubric, so what passes at one church can need review at another.

Pastor rulings become precedent.

When a pastor approves or rejects something, they can save it as a ruling. Similar claims later are judged with that ruling in view, so the standard grows from the church's judgment, not the tool's.

Every decision is logged.

Who approved what, and when, is kept in an audit trail. Edits send a commitment back to draft until a pastor approves it again.

For developers

Guardrails for every church tool.

Any other church tool can call Plumbline before it publishes AI-written content. One request returns the verdict, each claim, the verse lookups and the evidence.
